# OSED User Guide for Principals and School Leaders

> *Please note that the OSED (and associated documents) is in pilot form and will be reviewed for development and improvement throughout the year.*

## 1. Log In

- Access the OSED via the link on the front page of the Oxlip Hub, or go directly to the OSED website: [osed.oxlip.uk](https://osed.oxlip.uk/)
- Select **Sign in with Microsoft**.

## 2. Using the School Dashboard

- The dashboard is the starting point for all evaluation activity.
- This should be completed on a termly basis.
- The Principal will decide how the dashboard is completed e.g. collegiately with all SLT or whether individuals will complete for their leadership areas.
- Ensure the correct school and academic year are selected. You should only be able to see the school(s) that you work with.
  - NB. If that is not the case, please email <estoneham@oxlip.uk>
- All three terms — Autumn, Spring and Summer — appear side by side for the year you have selected. Complete the column for the current term.
- For each Ofsted area, select the judgement box and choose the overall judgement from the five ratings offered.
- Press **Save** before leaving the page.
- This judgement automatically feeds into the Evaluation section.

## 3. Using the Termly School Evaluation

- Navigate to the **Evaluation** tab on the left menu.
- This will be completed on a termly basis.
- The leader with responsibility for that area (or leaders depending on the setup in the school) should complete this termly school evaluation.

> NB. The Principal has the responsibility for reviewing the judgements and commentary at the end of the process.

- Check that the correct academic year and term are selected at the top of the page.
- The judgement you selected on the dashboard will already be populated.
- Complete the **Commentary** (your 300-word evidence summary) and **Next steps** (your 300-word priorities and next steps) for each area.
- Use the [Inspection Toolkit](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a2abaf3c39255c595b50722/Schools_inspection_toolkit_for_use_from_September_2026.pdf) and [Oxlip Supporting Tools](https://coplestonsuffolkschuk.sharepoint.com/:f:/r/sites/OxlipCentral/Shared%20Documents/School%20Improvement%20Folders/Trust%20SI%20Framework/Framework/PILOT%20phase%20OSED%20Supporting%20Tools?csf=1&web=1&e=92fCEX) to guide your evaluation.
- Use the guidance in steps 9 and 10 below to structure your writing.
- Press **Save** before exiting.

## 4. [Understanding the Supporting Tools](https://coplestonsuffolkschuk.sharepoint.com/:x:/r/sites/OxlipCentral/Shared%20Documents/School%20Improvement%20Folders/Trust%20SI%20Framework/Framework/PILOT%20phase%20OSED%20Supporting%20Tools/Updated%20Leadership%20and%20Governance%202026.xlsx?d=w06772f0a28774123b4e2c41e504f52b4&csf=1&web=1&e=bDcNM2)

- Each judgement area within the supporting tool is taken directly from the grading of each evaluation area within the Ofsted Inspection Toolkit.
- The supporting tool is designed to help you evaluate whether a grade is met and to support you in writing your commentary and next steps.
- The columns should be used together rather than in isolation.

### 4.1 Judgement Area

- The judgement area is extracted from the grading section of the Inspection Toolkit. It is each separate bullet point that appears within that grade. For expected standard and strong standard, all bullet points within that standard must be met.

### 4.2 Key Questions

- The key questions are prompts to help you interpret the judgement area and consider what inspectors might be looking for. They help you move from the wording of the bullet point to the reality of practice in your school.

### 4.3 Suggested Evidence

- Suggested evidence describes the type and quality of evidence that would typically indicate that the standard is met. Use it to compare the evidence available in your school with the evidence that might reasonably be expected if the standard were secure.
- It should be used as a guide rather than a checklist and alongside the key questions. You are not expected to have every piece of evidence listed. Instead, use a range of evidence sources to determine whether the standard is met securely and how well your evidence sits alongside the quality described in the suggested evidence.

### 4.4 Where schools might source this evidence

- This provides examples of documents, reports, records or activities that may help you to locate the evidence. The examples are not exhaustive and schools may hold similar evidence in different formats.
- This column is intended to help you locate evidence, not determine your judgement.

### 4.5 RAG rating

- After considering the judgement area, key questions and available evidence, determine whether your evidence is red, amber or green.
- Your RAG rating should reflect the security of the evidence rather than your confidence in the school.

| RAG | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Red** | Insufficient evidence to meet the standard |
| **Amber** | Partial evidence — you believe the standard is met but some evidence is insecure |
| **Green** | Comprehensive evidence supports your judgement, typically demonstrable from multiple sources |

- Amber and green indicate that the criterion is met. A red will trigger the consideration of a lower grade.

### 4.6 Worked examples

- These provide examples of how commentary and next steps can be written using evidence to justify the RAG rating. They are intended to demonstrate the style and level of evaluation expected rather than provide model answers to copy.

### 4.7 Bringing it together

- Read the bullet point and consider exactly what is being required.
- Use the key questions to explore what this might look like in practice and whether leaders can clearly articulate and evidence it.
- Review the suggested evidence and compare this with the evidence available within your school.
- Use the suggested evidence sources to locate and gather relevant information.
- Triangulate evidence from multiple sources to determine how securely the criterion is met.
- Apply a RAG rating based on the security, consistency and quality of the evidence.
- Use the strongest evidence to write evaluative commentary that explains why the judgement has been reached and what impact is evident.
- Identify any gaps, inconsistencies or opportunities for improvement when writing next steps.

## 5. Complete an In-Depth Review

- Each Ofsted area receives a detailed review at least once per year.
- The leader with responsibility for that area (or leaders depending on the setup in the school) should complete this in-depth review.

> NB. The Principal has the responsibility for reviewing the judgements and commentary at the end of the process.

| OSED Round | Term | Ofsted areas |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Summer 2 — Autumn 1 | Inclusion; Leadership & Governance; Achievement; Early Years / P16 |
| 2 | Autumn 2 — Spring 1 | Inclusion; Safeguarding; Attendance & Behaviour |
| 3 | Spring 2 — Summer 1 | Inclusion; Curriculum & Teaching; Personal Development and Wellbeing |

> NB. The rounds above are the School Improvement cycle. In the system itself, in-depth reviews are held against the **academic year** rather than a term, and every area is available from the dropdown at any point in the year. Early Years and Post-16 appear as two separate areas.

> NB. Principals may wish for areas to receive an additional in-depth review outside of this cycle due to school context and needs. This is possible. Please discuss this with Yvonne/Ele. Where a school has an imminent Ofsted, it is recommended that all areas are completed. Where an area is revisited within the same academic year, you are updating the existing in-depth review for that area rather than starting a second one alongside it.

The in-depth review is a two-step process.

### Step 1 — RAG-Rate Each Statement

- You will see every criterion within the *expected standard*. For each statement, select:
  - **Red** — insufficient evidence to meet the standard
  - **Amber** — partial evidence, you believe the standard is met but some evidence is insecure
  - **Green** — comprehensive evidence supports your judgement, which is typically demonstrable from multiple sources
- To meet the standard, you should be able to demonstrate that each statement within that standard is securely met.
- The system uses your RAG ratings to decide whether you are asked to rate further statements, and to conclude your grade:
  - Any **red** at expected standard concludes **Needs Attention**, and no further statements are shown.
  - **Amber**, with no reds, at expected standard concludes **Expected Standard**.
  - All **green** at expected standard opens the **strong standard** statements, and all green at strong standard opens the **exceptional** statements.
- Only the expected standard, strong standard and exceptional statements are RAG-rated. You are never asked to RAG-rate Needs Attention or Urgent Improvement statements.
- Every statement in a set must be rated before the system will conclude a grade.
- **Safeguarding works differently.** It has a single set of statements and concludes **Met** or **Not Met**: any red concludes Not Met.

> NB. If an in-depth review concludes a judgement grade that is different from the current grade for that area, an alert appears on the **Evaluation** tab headed *"In-depth review grades differ"*. It shows you both grades side by side (e.g. *in-depth grade: Needs Attention vs current: Expected*) and gives you an **"Over-write with in-depth grade"** button, plus an optional box to record a reason. Clicking this swaps the evaluation grade to the in-depth one, and that flows straight through to the school dashboard.

- Click **Save**, or save and continue to commentary *(this is your preference depending on how much time you have)*.

### Step 2 — Commentary and Next Steps

- After RAG-rating, continue to commentary.
- You are asked to write up the statements for the grade you have reached, rather than every statement you rated on the way to it. Where the review concludes Needs Attention, you write up the expected standard statements, so that each red and amber is explained.
- Each bullet point will now be expanded to include drop downs for key questions, suggested evidence and example write up. These are extracted from the supporting tools.
- Suggested evidence should be used as a guide rather than a checklist. Your judgement should be based on the quality and security of evidence available, not the number of evidence sources referenced.
- This is followed by a text box for your commentary and a text box for your next steps. **Both are limited to 150 words** on the in-depth review; the 300-word limits apply to the termly evaluation.
- Where the review concludes **Needs Attention**, you are also shown the Needs Attention statements as a prompt — *in addition, does one or more of the following apply?* — with a box to record your response.
- Press **Save** before leaving the page.

## 6. Using the Risk Register

- Navigate to the **Risk** tab on the left menu.
- The register is reviewed and updated on a termly basis, alongside the dashboard and evaluation.
- The Principal is responsible for the school's register. Where a COO, Business Manager or Site Manager holds an area of risk, they may draft an entry, but the Principal remains accountable for what appears on the register.
- Ensure the correct school, academic year and term are selected before you start.
- The **Show** filter (All risks / Yellow and above / Amber and above / Red only) changes what is displayed on your screen. It does not remove anything from the register.

> NB. A risk is a long-lived record, not a termly form. Once logged, it stays on your register across terms until it is actively closed. Each term you revisit what is already there rather than starting again.

### 6.1 How a risk is scored

- Every risk is scored on two things only: **Impact** (Low / Medium / High) and **Likelihood** (Unlikely / Possible / Highly probable).
- The band and its colour are then derived from the Trust's Impact × Likelihood matrix, which is shown at the top of the page. You do not choose the colour, and it cannot be over-ridden.

|  | Unlikely | Possible | Highly probable |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **High impact** | Monitor | High priority | Critical |
| **Medium impact** | Low risk | Medium priority | High priority |
| **Low impact** | Low risk | Low risk | Monitor |

| Band | Colour | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Critical** | Red | Immediate action |
| **High priority** | Amber | Mitigate as soon as possible |
| **Medium priority** | Yellow | Monitor |
| **Monitor** | Yellow | Monitor |
| **Low risk** | Green | Under control |

> NB. There are four colours on the risk register, not three: Yellow sits between Green and Amber. This is a different scale from the 1–5 evaluation grades used elsewhere in the OSED — an amber risk is not a quality judgement about the school.

- Score the risk **as it stands now, with your existing controls in place**. Not the worst case if nothing were done, and not the position you expect to reach once your mitigation is complete.
- Impact should reflect the consequence to pupils, staff, statutory compliance or the school's finances if the risk materialised. Likelihood should reflect your honest assessment for the current term rather than the whole year.
- Apply the matrix consistently term on term. Where a rating changes, it should be because the risk has changed, not because a different person completed it.

### 6.2 Logging a new risk

Use **Add a risk** at the foot of the page.

- **Risk** — name the risk and its consequence in one line, e.g. *"Perimeter fencing damaged, site security compromised"*. Write the risk rather than the incident: "three gates left unlocked" is an issue; the risk is what could follow from it. One risk per entry.
- **Category** — choose from the fourteen Trust categories: the nine OSED evaluation areas, and the five Operations & Resources domains (Finance & ICFP, HR & Workforce, Estates & Operations, IT, Governance & Compliance).
- The category determines where the risk is discussed. Evaluation areas route to **SIV**; Operations & Resources domains route to **TFORS**. There is no routing field to complete — the tag shown next to the category on your register tells you where it goes.
- **Impact** and **Likelihood** — the **Scores as** line updates as you choose, so you can see the band before you save.
- **Mitigation** — what is already in place and what is being done, written so that a reader outside the school can tell whether the risk is being managed. 150 words maximum; text is cut off at the limit.
- **Owner** — the role accountable for the mitigation, e.g. *COO, Principal, Site Manager*. A role rather than a name.
- **Review point** — the half-term at which you will next look at it, e.g. *Aut 2, Spring 1*.
- Press **Add to register**. The risk is created, and its first rating is recorded against the term selected at the top of the page.

> NB. Once a risk has been added, the title, category, mitigation, owner and review point cannot be edited from the Risk tab — only the term rating and the term note. Please check these before pressing Add to register. If something needs correcting afterwards, contact the Central Team, who can amend it.

### 6.3 Reviewing your register each term

- The page opens on the risks that are already open. Work through these first, then add anything new below.
- For each open risk, confirm or change **Impact** and **Likelihood** for the term now selected, and use **Note for this term** to record what explains the rating — what has changed, what has been done, what you are watching.
- Press **Save ratings** before leaving the page.
- Each row then shows the colour for the term, the trend against the previous rating (**Improving**, **Persisting** or **Worsening**), last term's colour, the owner and the review point.

> NB. **Persisting** appears only where a risk is unchanged at Amber or Red. An unchanged Green is not flagged.

- An open risk you do not rate shows **Not yet rated this term**. It has no colour, produces no trend, does not reach the CFO for sign-off and cannot escalate. An unrated risk is invisible to the Trust rather than assumed to be under control, so every open risk needs a rating every term, even where nothing has changed.

### 6.4 What happens after you log or re-rate a risk

- Every new risk, and every rating you add or change, is flagged **Awaiting QA** on your own register. You can see that flag on your Risk tab even though the QA screen itself is not visible to you. Once it has been signed off, the row shows **QA'd**.
- The **CFO** quality assures the register across the Trust from a separate Risk QA screen, which lists everything awaiting sign-off, and signs off each entry individually.
- QA is a check that the risk is described clearly, categorised correctly and scored consistently with the framework and with other schools. It does not change your rating: where the CFO takes a different view, that is a conversation with you rather than a silent edit.
- If you change the impact or likelihood of a risk that has already been signed off, the sign-off is cleared and the entry returns to the CFO's queue. Editing only the note does not.
- Risks are then discussed at **TFORS** or **SIV**, according to their category.
- Any risk sitting at **Red** for the term appears on the **Trust Dashboard** as a *Risk — exception report*, showing the school, risk, category, impact, likelihood, colour, trend and owner. Trustees see this, and it is used in board packs. Red risks escalate to the **Finance, Audit and Risk Committee**.
- Only Red escalates under the currently agreed rule. An Amber that persists across terms is visible on your register and to the CFO, and will be picked up at TFORS or SIV, but does not automatically go to the Committee.
- Nothing else on your register is reported to trustees automatically. The full register stays on your school's Risk tab.

### 6.5 How a risk comes off the register

- The only way a risk leaves the open list is by being closed. Nothing is deleted, and nothing drops off because a term has passed or because it was not rated.
- Open **Close a risk**, find the risk, give a **reason** for closing it (required, 150 words maximum) and press **Close this risk**.
- The risk moves to **Closed risks**, greyed out, showing its last rating, the closure date and your reason. It stays there permanently — the Committee needs to see what has been closed and why, rather than a register that has quietly become shorter.
- A closure is quality assured in the same way: it shows **Awaiting QA** until the CFO signs it off.
- Close a risk when the risk itself has gone: the work is complete, the contract is let, the building is repaired, the risk has transferred elsewhere.
- Do not close a risk because it has been quiet this term. Where a risk has reduced, re-rate it — a move from High/Possible to Low/Unlikely shows as **Improving** and keeps the history visible.
- There is no undo, and a closed risk cannot be reopened. If a closed risk returns, log it again as a new risk, which starts a fresh history from that term.

### 6.6 What a well-completed register looks like

- One risk per entry, named so that someone outside the school can understand it without explanation.
- The rating reflects the position now, with current controls in place.
- The mitigation says what is being done and by when, rather than that the risk is "being monitored".
- The rating moves when the risk moves, and the note explains why.
- The register is short enough to be real: the risks the school is genuinely managing, rather than every risk that could be imagined.

## 7. Sign-off

- Prior to submission, the Principal is responsible for checking the dashboard and reading commentaries to ensure the Principal is in agreement with self-evaluation, evidence base and written style.
- The Principal may wish to set an internal deadline to give them sufficient time for this quality assurance before the OSED closes.
- The OSED closes at the end of day on the deadline communicated in the SI calendar.

## 8. What happens next?

### Quality Assurance

- Yvonne and Ele review all submissions, including evaluations and in-depth reviews. They then prepare supplementary questions to explore areas where clarification, challenge or additional evidence may be required.
- The supplementary questions might be to seek clarification, challenge, or request further evidence.
- These could relate to overall evaluation and in-depth reviews.
- Schools receive these as a separate document but respond to them by updating directly onto the OSED online system, using the **Reflection** tab (*Reflection on QA & Feedback*), which holds a box for each area for the academic year.
- QA meetings are then held to discuss the feedback and agree submission to governance.

### Outputs and Reporting

The OSED automatically generates:

- **School Dashboard** (shared with LAB) — a colour-coded, numerical overview of the school's evaluation in each Ofsted area.
- **School Progress** — each area across Autumn, Spring and Summer for the year selected, so movement within the year can be seen at a glance.
- **Trust Dashboard** (shared with Trustees) — a Trust-wide grid of every school against every Ofsted area for the term, with arrows showing movement against the previous term, together with the risk exception report and the Operations & Resources summary.
- **SEF-Ready Summaries** — the 300-word evaluations and next steps are written to be used directly in the SEF, removing the need for a separate SES.

## 9. Writing the evaluation or in-depth review commentary

- Write in a professional, formal tone.
- The commentary should draw on the Inspection Toolkit, Oxlip Supporting Tools and the school's evidence base.
- Explain the judgement reached, the evidence that supports it, how leaders know, and the impact that is evident.
- Explain **why** the judgement has been reached, rather than providing a narrative or summary of activity.
- Draw on triangulated evidence from a range of sources, such as data, monitoring activity, documentation and stakeholder feedback, referring to this selectively rather than listing everything available.
- Focus on impact, consistency and the security of evidence.
- Use precise examples to demonstrate how provision is working in practice and the difference it is making.
- Where relevant, explain how leaders have responded when provision has fallen short and the impact of those actions.
- Where evidence or practice is not fully secure, explain why the judgement remains appropriate and identify any limitations or inconsistencies.
- Recognise that improvement does not automatically mean a standard has been met. Evaluate current practice against the criteria.
- Avoid descriptive accounts of processes, lists of monitoring activities, generic statements such as "teaching is strong" or "students do well", and claims that are not supported by evidence.
- Word limits are strict and text will be cut off at the word limit. A counter beneath each box shows how many words you have used.
- Example evaluations are provided within the supporting tools.

## 10. Writing about next steps and to progress

- Write in a professional, formal tone.
- Next steps should follow directly from your evaluation and identify the key actions required to strengthen provision further.
- Focus on the gaps, inconsistencies or areas that prevent a stronger judgement from being awarded.
- Make next steps specific, strategic and improvement-focused rather than generic or activity-based.
- Where appropriate, explain how progress will be monitored, reviewed or evaluated.
- For amber judgements, focus on securing the current standard by addressing the areas where evidence or practice remains less secure.
- For green judgements, use the next standard as your starting point and identify what would need to be more consistent, embedded or evidenced to move towards that higher standard.
- Avoid next steps that simply suggest more monitoring or more of the same activity unless these address a clearly identified gap.
- Effective next steps explain not only what will happen next, but why this will strengthen provision and support progress towards the next standard.
- Word limits are strict and text will be cut off at the word limit.
- Example next steps are provided within the supporting tools.
